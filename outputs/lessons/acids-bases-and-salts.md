## What are Acids and Bases?

### What are Acids and Bases?

**Acid** (a substance that tastes sour and can burn the skin).

Imagine a lemon. It tastes very sour. This sour taste is an acid. Acids are also in vinegar. They can burn your skin if you touch them.

**Base** (a substance that tastes bitter and feels slippery).

Imagine soap. It tastes bitter. It also feels slippery on your hands. This is a base. Bases are in cleaning products. They help clean dirty things.

**Example:**
1.  Taste a small amount of lemon juice. It tastes sour. This is an acid.
2.  Touch some soap. It feels slippery. This is a base.

## Testing Acids and Bases

### Testing Acids and Bases

We cannot taste acids or bases to know what they are. It is dangerous. We use a special tool called **Litmus paper** (a paper that changes color to show if something is an acid or a base).

**Litmus paper turns red in acids.**
It turns blue in bases.

**Example:**
1.  Take a piece of blue litmus paper.
2.  Dip it into lemon juice.
3.  The paper turns red. Lemon juice is an acid.
4.  Take a new piece of blue litmus paper.
5.  Dip it into soap water.
6.  The paper turns blue. Soap water is a base.

This test is safe. It is common in laboratories. We use it to identify acids and bases without tasting them.

## How Acids and Bases React

### How Acids and Bases React

When an acid and a base mix, they neutralize each other. This means they react to form a new, neutral substance. This reaction is called neutralization.

**Example: Mixing Vinegar and Baking Soda**

1.  Take a small cup.
2.  Pour some vinegar (an acid) into the cup.
3.  Add some baking soda (a base) to the vinegar.
4.  You will see bubbles. This is the reaction.
5.  The acid and the base react to form a salt and water.

The result of this reaction is a salt. A salt is a common substance found in food and on the table. The acid and the base are no longer strong. They have become neutral.

## Uses of Salts in Daily Life

### Uses of Salts in Daily Life

**Food**
We use salt in food. It makes food taste good. It also helps keep food fresh for a long time. This is called **preservation** (keeping food fresh for a long time). Salt stops the growth of bacteria. Bacteria are tiny living things that can make food bad. We put salt on meat to keep it from spoiling. We also use salt in pickles.

**Medicine**
Salts are in many medicines. They help us feel better when we are sick. Toothpaste is a good example. Toothpaste has salt. It helps clean our teeth and keeps our mouth healthy.

**Cleaning**
Salts are in cleaning products. They help us clean dirty things. We use salt to clean the kitchen sink. It helps remove stains. It makes the sink shine.

**The Human Body**
Salts are very important for the human body. Our body needs salt to work correctly. It helps our nerves and muscles move. We need it to stay healthy.

## Summary and Next Steps

### Summary and Next Steps

**Acids** (a substance that tastes sour and can burn the skin) and **bases** (a substance that tastes bitter and feels slippery) are common in our daily lives. We can identify them using **Litmus paper** (a special paper that changes color). The paper turns red for acids and blue for bases.

When an acid and a base mix, they react. This reaction is called **neutralization** (a process where an acid and a base cancel each other out). The result of this reaction is a **salt** (a white powder used in food and cleaning). For example, vinegar (an acid) and baking soda (a base) react to form a salt and water.

Salts are very useful. We use them in food to make it taste good and keep it fresh. We also use them in medicine and cleaning products. Now you know how to identify acids and bases and how they form salts.
