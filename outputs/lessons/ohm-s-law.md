## What is Ohm's Law?

Ohm's law is a basic rule that connects voltage, current, and resistance. It helps us understand how electricity moves in a circuit.

**Voltage** is the push that makes electricity flow. Think of it as the force that moves electric charge through a wire. It provides the energy needed to start the movement.

**Current** is the flow of electricity. It is the movement of electric charge through a path. We measure current in amperes, often called amps.

**Resistance** is the opposition to the flow of electricity. It is the force that slows down the current. It acts like a barrier to the movement of charge.

Ohm's law states that current is directly proportional to voltage and inversely proportional to resistance. This means that if voltage goes up, current goes up. If resistance goes up, current goes down.

Let us use a water pipe to understand this. Imagine a water pipe connected to a water tank. The water tank is full of water.

1. The water in the tank has pressure. This pressure is like **voltage**. It pushes the water out of the pipe.
2. The water flowing through the pipe is like **current**. It is the movement of water.
3. If the pipe is very wide, the water flows easily. If the pipe is very narrow, the water flows slowly. The narrowness of the pipe is like **resistance**.

This example shows how voltage, current, and resistance are connected in a simple system.

## The Formula and Its Parts

Ohm's law uses a simple math rule to connect voltage, current, and resistance. This section shows the formula and how to use it.

The formula for Ohm's law is V = I * R. This equation connects the three main parts of an electric circuit. It shows how they work together to make electricity flow.

**V** stands for Voltage. Voltage is the force that pushes the electricity through the wire. It is like the pressure in a water pipe.

**I** stands for Current. Current is the actual flow of electricity. It is the movement of electric charge through the path.

**R** stands for Resistance. Resistance is the opposition to the flow of electricity. It is the force that slows down the movement.

A **formula** is a short way to write a rule using numbers and letters. It helps us solve problems quickly. We use the formula to find the missing value.

Imagine a water pipe with a narrow section. The pressure (V) is high, but the flow (I) is low because the narrow section (R) resists the flow.

Here is how this example works:
1. The water pressure is high. This represents the high voltage (V).
2. The water flow is slow. This represents the low current (I).
3. The pipe is narrow. This represents the high resistance (R).

This example shows that high resistance can limit the current even when the voltage is high.

## Calculating Current, Voltage, and Resistance

Now you can use the formula to find the missing value. You can calculate the current, voltage, or resistance in a circuit.

To find current (I), divide voltage (V) by resistance (R): I = V / R. Current is the flow of electricity. Voltage is the push. Resistance is the block. To get the flow, you divide the push by the block. This gives you the amount of current.

To find voltage (V), multiply current (I) by resistance (R): V = I * R. Voltage is the push. Current is the flow. Resistance is the block. To get the push, you multiply the flow by the block. This gives you the voltage.

To find resistance (R), divide voltage (V) by current (I): R = V / I. Resistance is the block. Voltage is the push. Current is the flow. To get the block, you divide the push by the flow. This gives you the resistance.

Always use the correct units: Volts (V), Amperes (A), and Ohms (Ω). **Amperes** is the unit for current. **Ohms** is the unit for resistance. You must use these units in your math. This keeps your answer clear and correct.

Let us look at a real example. Imagine a 10-volt battery is connected to a 5-ohm resistor. We want to find the current.

1.  Identify the known values. The voltage is 10 volts. The resistance is 5 ohms.
2.  Use the formula for current. The formula is I = V / R.
3.  Substitute the values into the formula. I = 10 / 5.
4.  Do the division. 10 divided by 5 is 2.
5.  State the final answer. The current is 2 amperes.

This example shows how to use the formula to find the current in a simple circuit.

## Real-World Application: A Light Bulb

Ohm's law is used to design and understand electrical devices like light bulbs. Engineers use it to make sure devices work safely. A **light bulb** is a device that uses electricity to make light. It has a wire inside that gets hot and glows.

A light bulb has a specific resistance that determines how much current flows when connected to a voltage source. The bulb has a wire inside it. This wire has resistance. The resistance stops the flow of electricity. It controls how much current goes through the bulb.

By knowing the voltage of a power source and the bulb's resistance, you can predict the brightness of the bulb. Voltage is the push. Resistance is the block. The push and the block work together. They decide how bright the bulb will be. If the push is high, the bulb gets bright. If the push is low, the bulb gets dim.

If the resistance is too low, too much current can flow, which can damage the bulb or the power source. This is a safety rule. If the block is too small, the flow can be too fast. This can make the bulb get too hot. It can also hurt the power source. We must keep the resistance at the right level.

A **Power Source** is a thing that gives electricity to a circuit. It provides the push for the electricity to move.

Here is a real example of a light bulb in a car.

1. A car has a 12-volt battery. This is the power source.
2. The battery connects to a headlight bulb.
3. The bulb has 6 ohms of resistance.
4. We use Ohm's law to find the current. I = V / R. I = 12 / 6. The current is 2 amperes.
5. The current is 2 amperes. This makes the bulb glow brightly.

This example shows how Ohm's law helps us understand how a light bulb works.
